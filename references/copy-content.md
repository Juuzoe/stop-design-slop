# Catalog 6/7 — Copy & content (#184–251)

Headlines, AI sentence constructions, word blacklist, CTAs, section copy, trust signals, testimonials, FAQ, about pages, blog, content hygiene. Severity: **instant** / **strong** / **mild** (see SKILL.md).

Master test for this file: **the swap test** — if a sentence still works with a competitor's logo on the page, it's slop. And **the read-aloud test** — would the founder say this sentence out loud to a customer?

## Headlines & value proposition

**184. "Verb your Noun" headline formula** (instant)
Tell: hero H1 = imperative marketing verb + "your" + abstract noun — "Supercharge Your Workflow", "Elevate Your Business", "Transform Your Productivity."
Why: LLMs average thousands of SaaS heroes into this template; the verb comes from the AI verb set and the noun names no actual product or outcome.
Fix: name mechanism + object in plain words — "Turn your Stripe exports into investor-ready reports" (cf. Stripe's "Financial infrastructure for the internet").

**185. "The future of X" headline** (instant)
Tell: "The Future of Work Is Here", "The future of team collaboration."
Why: grandiose category claim with zero information — the statistical default when the model doesn't know what the product does.
Fix: describe what it does today for whom — "Payroll for restaurants that run on tips."

**186. "X, reimagined" appositive headline** (instant)
Tell: single noun + comma + past participle — "Invoicing, reimagined.", "Meetings, simplified.", "Email, reinvented."
Why: borrowed Apple cadence that every AI builder emits; promises novelty without stating any.
Fix: state the reimagining itself — "Invoices that chase themselves: auto-reminders until you're paid."

**187. Twin staccato imperative hero** (instant)
Tell: two 2–3 word parallel imperatives with periods — "Build faster. Ship smarter.", "Work less. Achieve more."
Why: rhythm-first, meaning-second construction LLMs produce when asked for "punchy"; interchangeable across any product.
Fix: one concrete claim with a real object — "Deploy a Postgres branch in 300ms."

**188. "All-in-one platform" positioning** (instant)
Tell: "The all-in-one platform for modern teams", "One platform. Endless possibilities."
Why: claims everything, proves nothing — the single most common AI-generated value prop.
Fix: pick the one job you're best at and lead with it; name the tools you actually replace — "Replaces Trello + Slack threads + weekly status emails."

**189. Category-free abstraction headline** (instant)
Tell: hero never says what the product IS — "Scale without limits", "Build the future", "Unlock your potential."
Why: aspirational vapor from averaging headlines; the visitor can't answer "what is this?" after the first screen.
Fix: the one-sentence-value-prop test — a stranger reading only H1+subhead must be able to say what it is, who it's for, why it's different; add the category noun ("An uptime monitor that...").

**190. Echo subheadline** (instant)
Tell: subhead restates the H1 in buzzword long-form — "Our platform helps teams of all sizes streamline their workflow, boost productivity, and achieve more."
Why: LLMs treat the subhead as elaboration, not new information — the triple-buzzword restatement.
Fix: the subhead carries the mechanism or proof the H1 couldn't fit — "Connects to your GitHub, flags the 5% of PRs that cause 80% of incidents."

**191. "Whether you're X or Y" audience hedge** (strong)
Tell: "Whether you're a solo founder or a Fortune 500 team, Acme scales with you."
Why: documented AI inclusiveness template that avoids choosing a customer, so nobody feels seen.
Fix: pick the primary user and name them — "Built for agencies juggling 20+ client accounts"; let secondary audiences self-select.

**192. "AI-powered" as the entire value prop** (strong)
Tell: "AI-powered insights for modern teams", "Harness the power of AI" — AI is the subject, benefit unstated.
Why: "AI-powered" describes implementation, not value, and now signals template output.
Fix: state what the AI does concretely — "Drafts your reply in your past tone; you edit and send."

## AI sentence constructions

**193. "It's not just X — it's Y" negation pivot** (instant)
Tell: "It's not just a CRM — it's your revenue engine.", "This isn't software. It's a movement."
Why: the most-cited AI construction (negative parallelism — Wikipedia's most prominent tell; 3+ per page = slop threshold); manufactures fake depth via contrast.
Fix: cut the negation, keep only a specific second half — "A CRM that writes the follow-up email for you"; at most one deliberate contrast per page.

**194. "Say goodbye to X / say hello to Y"** (strong)
Tell: "Say goodbye to spreadsheets. Say hello to clarity." (also "Stop guessing. Start growing.")
Why: paired-imperative contrast template — another face of the not-X-but-Y tic AI leans on for drama.
Fix: keep the pain, drop the greeting-card frame — "Your budget lives in 11 spreadsheets. Put it in one place that updates itself."

**195. "No X. No Y. Just Z."** (strong)
Tell: "No code. No hassle. Just results.", "No fluff. No filler. Just answers."
Why: documented staccato-triplet tell; scans as rhythm, contains one claim at most.
Fix: keep only the strongest negation and make it verifiable — "No credit card, no sales call — the free tier is the real product."

**196. Rule-of-three everywhere** (strong)
Tell: every heading, list, and sentence resolves in triads — "Fast, reliable, and secure", "Plan, execute, and optimize" — plus three cards, three steps, three tiers.
Why: LLMs are tuned toward tricolon completeness; more than one polished triplet per 200 words is a measured AI threshold. (Layout version: #25.)
Fix: break the count on purpose — two blunt items or four uneven ones; cut the weakest member of every triad.

**197. Trailing "-ing" significance clause** (strong)
Tell: sentences end with a participial value-claim — "...integrates with Slack, ensuring seamless collaboration across your organization."
Why: a Wikipedia-documented AI habit — bolting hollow analysis onto facts via present participles.
Fix: end at the fact, or make the consequence concrete in its own sentence — "Messages sync to Slack in under a second."

**198. "In today's fast-paced world" opener** (strong)
Tell: section or blog opens "In today's fast-paced digital world...", "In the age of AI...", "In an era where..."
Why: empty-orientation cliché models emit as a default throat-clear; humans edit it out.
Fix: delete the sentence entirely — start with the claim or the problem: "Your team wastes 6 hours a week in status meetings."

**199. Em-dash density** (mild)
Tell: multiple em dashes per paragraph — "Acme is fast — really fast — and secure."
Why: AI's em-dash rate roughly doubles the human baseline, and readers now flag it; only density matters (>2 per 100 words).
Fix: keep em dashes you'd defend; convert the rest to commas, parentheses, or two sentences.

**200. "Not only... but also..."** (mild)
Tell: "Not only does Acme save time, but it also reduces errors."
Why: padding construction on every AI-tell list — doubles length without doubling meaning.
Fix: two plain sentences or one with "and" — "Acme saves time and catches errors before they ship."

**201. "From X to Y" fake-range construction** (mild)
Tell: "From startups to enterprises, from marketing to engineering, Acme has you covered."
Why: documented AI range template that simulates comprehensiveness without evidence.
Fix: name 2–3 real customers across the range instead of the abstract span.

**202. Hedging modals in sales copy** (mild)
Tell: "can help you improve...", "may reduce costs", "designed to potentially streamline."
Why: AI hedges to stay safe; marketing that won't commit reads as generated.
Fix: commit where you have evidence, scope where you don't — "Cuts render times 35–60% on codebases over 100k lines."

**203. Metronomic sentence rhythm** (mild)
Tell: every sentence 15–20 words, same subject-verb-object shape, paragraph after paragraph.
Why: low sentence-length variance is one of the few statistically measurable AI fingerprints.
Fix: vary deliberately — follow a long compound sentence with a three-word punch; read aloud and mark where you'd breathe.

## The word blacklist (grep the rendered text)

**204. Marketing-verb density** (strong)
Tell: unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, supercharge, streamline, harness, revolutionize, transform clustered across headings.
Why: the documented AI marketing verb set; density >1 per screen reads as generated.
Fix: swap for the literal action — "Supercharge your productivity" → "Cut invoice prep from 2 hours to 10 minutes."

**205. "Seamless/seamlessly"** (strong)
Tell: "seamless integration", "seamlessly connects with your tools."
Why: flagship AI adjective (top of every 2024–2026 blacklist); an unfalsifiable quality claim.
Fix: replace with the evidence — "Two-way Salesforce sync every 60 seconds, no field mapping."

**206. "Effortless/effortlessly"** (strong)
Tell: "Effortlessly manage your projects", "Effortless expense tracking."
Why: stock AI adverb promising absence of work instead of describing the work removed.
Fix: name the effort eliminated — "No spreadsheets: receipts photograph themselves into categories."

**207. Speed hyperbole compounds** (strong)
Tell: "blazing-fast", "lightning-fast performance", "insanely fast."
Why: dev-tool AI slop signature — hyphenated speed compounds with no benchmark.
Fix: state the measurement and link it — "Cold start under 50ms (benchmarks below)."

**208. Edge-adjective cluster** (strong)
Tell: "cutting-edge", "next-level", "game-changing", "state-of-the-art", "best-in-class", "world-class", "industry-leading."
Why: statistically safe superlatives — self-awarded rank with no ranker.
Fix: replace rank claims with a differentiator only you can say — "The only editor with local-first sync (works on planes)."

**209. "In seconds" / "in minutes, not months"** (strong)
Tell: "Create stunning reports in seconds", "Launch in minutes, not months."
Why: AI's default time-compression template; almost never literally true, and the time-contrast is a known tic.
Fix: give the honest median time — "Most teams ship their first workflow in 25 minutes."

**210. "10x your X"** (strong)
Tell: "10x your productivity", "10x your content output."
Why: growth-hack multiplier with no baseline or source — a core AI hype token.
Fix: cite a real measured delta with denominator — "Writers on Acme publish 3.1× more posts/month (n=1,240, 2025 cohort)" — or drop the number.

**211. ChatGPT lexicon in site prose** (strong)
Tell: delve, tapestry, realm, landscape ("the ever-evolving landscape of e-commerce"), testament, pivotal, robust, multifaceted, holistic in About/blog/feature copy.
Why: the researched LLM focal-word set — words that spiked ~25× post-ChatGPT; >3 per 500 words is a reproducible slop threshold.
Fix: grep for the blacklist below and rewrite each hit as something a founder would say aloud.

**212. Self-praise UI adjectives** (strong)
Tell: "intuitive interface", "powerful features", "beautiful dashboards", "simple yet powerful."
Why: the product grading its own homework in the exact adjective set AI defaults to — describing feelings the visitor hasn't had yet.
Fix: show, then let a customer say it — screenshot + "Set up our 40-person rollout without reading a doc" — Dana R., IT lead (linked).

**213. "Actionable insights" / "data-driven decisions"** (strong)
Tell: feature blurbs ending "...so you can make data-driven decisions with actionable insights."
Why: the two most recycled B2B noun phrases in AI output — filler that names no insight.
Fix: name one actual insight the product surfaces — "See which onboarding step loses 30% of signups."

**214. "Everything you need" absolutes** (strong)
Tell: "Everything you need to grow your business", "The only tool you'll ever need."
Why: totalizing claims are AI's compression of value props, falsified by the visitor's own five-second scan.
Fix: scope it honestly and specifically — "Everything a 2-sided marketplace needs for payments: escrow, splits, refunds, 1099s."

**215. "Focus on what matters" cliché** (strong)
Tell: "...so you can focus on what matters most", "spend less time on busywork and more time doing what you love."
Why: the universal AI benefit-of-last-resort — appears when the model can't name the actual benefit.
Fix: name what they'll do with the saved time in their domain — "Close books by the 3rd instead of the 15th."

## CTAs & microcopy

**216. Generic CTA labels** (instant)
Tell: "Get Started" and "Learn More" as the labels on every button site-wide, regardless of what the click actually does. (The hero button-pair layout: #3.)
Why: the statistically dominant CTA labels in training data; generic labels signal no one thought about the actual next step.
Fix: label with the literal action and payoff — "Create your first form (free)", "See a 2-min demo", "Get a quote"; when two buttons coexist, make them different verbs, not synonyms.

**217. "Start your free trial today" filler CTA** (strong)
Tell: "Start your free trial today!", "Sign up now and transform your workflow."
Why: "today"/"now" are urgency filler AI appends by default; the copy sells signing up rather than what the click delivers.
Fix: sell the first session — "Import your data, see your dashboard in 3 minutes"; keep the free-trial fact as microcopy ("Free for 14 days").

**218. Exclamation saturation** (strong)
Tell: "Get started today!", "It's that easy!", "And the best part? It's free!" — multiple exclamation points per screen.
Why: AI compensates for empty claims with punctuation-level enthusiasm; uniform excitement flattens real emphasis.
Fix: near-zero exclamation budget (one per page, max); let specifics carry the energy.

**219. Emoji in headings and body copy** (instant)
Tell: "🚀 Launch Faster", "✨ Smart Features", "🎉 You're all set!", emoji-bulleted feature lists.
Why: ChatGPT decorates marketing output with the same small emoji set (🚀✨💡🎯🎉); density in headings is now a primary slop marker. (Emoji as icons: #144.)
Fix: strip emoji from headings entirely; if brand voice is playful, prove it with wit in the words, not pictographs.

**220. "Made with ❤️" signature** (mild)
Tell: "Made with ❤️ by the Acme team", "Crafted with love and coffee ☕."
Why: template-era affectation AI reproduces — warmth-signaling that replaced actual warmth.
Fix: be actually human instead — "Built by two ex-Shopify engineers in Ottawa. Email us: real@address."

**221. Default empty-state/error/404 microcopy** (mild)
Tell: "Oops! Something went wrong.", "No items found.", "Error 404: Page not found", "🎉 Success!" toasts.
Why: generator-default strings in the places where voice is cheapest to show; unowned microcopy marks an unowned product. (Missing 404 route: #263.)
Fix: write these in brand voice with a useful next step — 404: "That page moved or never existed. The three most-visited pages are..."; empty state: "No invoices yet — import from Stripe or create one (30 sec)."

## Section copy

**222. Feature-card copy formula** (strong)
Tell: 3 or 6 cards, each: icon + 2–3 word Title Case label + a two-line any-product blurb — "Real-Time Analytics / Get deep insights into your data with powerful, customizable dashboards." (Card layout: #26.)
Why: the canonical AI feature-grid copy; blurbs are averaged descriptions that fit every competitor.
Fix: one card = one specific capability + proof — "Anomaly alerts / Pings Slack when checkout conversion drops 2σ below the 28-day mean"; kill cards you can't make specific.

**223. Bolded-lead bullet formula** (strong)
Tell: lists of "**Fast:** Get results in record time. **Secure:** Your data is always protected. **Scalable:** Grows with your business."
Why: ChatGPT's signature bold-header-colon-explanation output pasted straight into the page.
Fix: bullets that each contain a number, name, or noun unique to your product; unbold decoration.

**224. Generic section headings** (strong)
Tell: "Features", "Benefits", "Why Choose Us?", "What We Offer", "What Our Customers Say", "Get In Touch."
Why: template scaffolding labels left as-is — AI names sections by function, humans name them by message.
Fix: make headings assertions — "Why Choose Us?" → "Why teams switch from Jira"; "Features" → "What you can ship with it."

**225. Generic 1-2-3 "How it works" copy** (strong)
Tell: "1. Sign Up — Create your account in seconds. 2. Connect — Link your tools. 3. Grow — Watch your business thrive." (Step layout: #15.)
Why: AI's default three-step onboarding narrative; step 3 is always an outcome fantasy, not a step.
Fix: describe the real first session with real nouns — "1. Paste your Stripe key. 2. Pick the 3 metrics you report to investors. 3. Get the Notion-ready digest every Monday 8am."

**226. Swap-logo interchangeability** (strong)
Tell: body copy passes the swap test — replace the logo with a competitor's and every sentence still works ("With Acme, you can streamline collaboration and boost productivity").
Why: AI averages the category's marketing; nothing ties copy to this product's actual behavior.
Fix: inject product-specific nouns per section — feature names, supported file types, limits, latencies, customer names; if a sentence could run on a rival's site, rewrite or cut.

**227. Uniform enthusiasm, zero tradeoffs** (strong)
Tell: every sentence sells, nothing is downplayed, no opinions, no "this isn't for you if...", no known limitations.
Why: emotional flatness + relentless positivity is a documented AI signature; humans have priorities and gripes.
Fix: add one honest constraint — "Not built for teams over 200 — we optimize for speed at small scale"; disqualification copy raises trust and conversion quality.

## Trust & proof claims

**228. Round-number herd claims** (instant)
Tell: "Trusted by 10,000+ teams worldwide", "Join thousands of happy customers" — with no logos, links, or plausibility. (Hero avatar-cluster version: #5; stat banner: #16.)
Why: unverifiable round-number herd claim — the single most parodied AI-site element; AI adds it reflexively even for products with zero users.
Fix: real checkable proof at whatever scale is true — "412 workspaces created this month", "Used by 214 YC companies" (linked); odd precise numbers outperform round ones. Remove until true.

**229. Implausible famous-logo bar** (instant)
Tell: Google, Microsoft, Netflix, Airbnb logos under "Trusted by industry leaders" on a product launched last week. (Fake made-up company logos: #14.)
Why: AI scaffolds a logo wall as standard anatomy and fills it with famous brands; one email to any named "customer" exposes it.
Fix: only logos with permission and a story; with none, show design partners, an accelerator badge, or open metrics ("2,400 GitHub stars") instead.

**230. "As seen on / Featured in" with no coverage** (strong)
Tell: press bar citing TechCrunch/Forbes/Product Hunt with no linked article (or the "coverage" is a paid directory).
Why: borrowed-authority template AI includes by default; one search catches it and poisons the whole page.
Fix: link every outlet to the actual piece; until real press exists, quote a named user instead.

**231. Unsourced star rating** (strong)
Tell: "4.9/5 stars" or five gold stars with no platform, no review count, no link.
Why: a rating is a summary of reviews; with no reviews named, it's decoration AI adds to look credible.
Fix: "4.7 on G2 (312 reviews)" linked to the G2 profile; if you have 6 reviews, quote the best one instead of averaging.

**232. Suspiciously precise unsourced percentages** (strong)
Tell: "Boost productivity by 47%", "Reduce churn by 32%" with no study, cohort, or link.
Why: models hallucinate authoritative-feeling numbers; precision without provenance is the tell.
Fix: attach the source or soften honestly — "Pilot teams (n=14) reported saving ~4 h/week (survey, Jan 2026)."

**233. Stage-contradicting claims** (strong)
Tell: "Join 50,000+ users" on a waitlist page; a "Beta" badge next to "Trusted by thousands"; "No credit card required" on a product with no billing at all. (Pre-launch social proof: #17.)
Why: AI writes each section independently against a fantasy company, so claims contradict the product's visible reality.
Fix: audit every claim against reality; early-stage honesty converts — "We launched 3 weeks ago. 214 people are already using it daily."

**234. Security theater phrases** (strong)
Tell: "Bank-level security", "Enterprise-grade encryption", "SOC 2 compliant" with no security page, DPA, or certificate anywhere.
Why: AI adds compliance vocabulary as decoration; buyers who care will look for the artifact and find nothing.
Fix: claim exactly what's true and link it — "AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit; SOC 2 Type II in progress (Q4) — security page →."

## Testimonials

**235. Central-casting testimonial names** (strong)
Tell: "Sarah Chen, Product Manager", "Sarah Johnson, Marketing Lead at TechFlow", "Michael Rodriguez, CEO of DataSync" — demographically balanced names, generic titles, ungoogleable companies. (Fake avatar photos: #143; card-wall layout: #18.)
Why: these exact names recur across AI-generated sites because models default to them; readers now pattern-match the roster.
Fix: real people with verifiable anchors — full name, real company (linked), LinkedIn/X handle, written permission; fewer real quotes beat six synthetic ones.

**236. Generic-praise testimonial quotes** (strong)
Tell: "This changed everything for our team!", "An absolute game-changer. Highly recommend!", "10/10 — best tool we've ever used."
Why: AI praise has no referent — no feature, no before/after, no friction; every quote is a five-star adjective.
Fix: quotes must contain a specific — "We switched from Asana and cut our Monday planning from 90 to 25 minutes"; interview customers and transcribe their actual words.

**237. Testimonial set uniformity** (mild)
Tell: all quotes are 2 sentences, same enthusiasm, same cadence, no dates, none mention problems or caveats.
Why: single-generation batch output; real review sets have variance, including lukewarm notes.
Fix: vary length and temperature; include one honest caveat quote ("Setup took an afternoon, but...") — mild negatives raise believability of the positives.

## FAQ

**238. FAQ questions nobody asked** (strong)
Tell: "What makes Acme different?", "Why should I choose Acme?", "Is Acme right for my business?" — marketing pitches disguised as questions. (Accordion layout: #21.)
Why: AI generates FAQs from the seller's perspective; real users ask about price, limits, migration, cancellation, data.
Fix: source questions from actual support tickets and sales calls; answer the awkward ones — "Can I export everything if I leave?" → "Yes, one-click JSON/CSV, no lock-in."

**239. "Yes! Absolutely." FAQ answers** (mild)
Tell: every answer opens "Yes! Absolutely..." then restates the question with buzzwords and no facts.
Why: AI answer style is affirmation + restatement + reassurance — zero informational density.
Fix: answer like a support engineer — first sentence is the fact ("Yes — export is on every plan, including free"), second is the caveat or link.

## About & company

**240. Mission-statement vapor** (strong)
Tell: "We believe in empowering businesses to unlock their full potential through innovative solutions", "Our mission is to democratize X."
Why: interchangeable corporate-speak AI produces for any company — no market, no opinion, no falsifiable belief.
Fix: state a specific, arguable conviction — "We think expense reports shouldn't exist, so we're deleting them one receipt-scan at a time."

**241. Origin-story boilerplate** (strong)
Tell: "Founded with a vision to revolutionize the industry...", "What started as a simple idea has grown into a passion for excellence" — no names, dates, or events. (Thin about-page structure: #53.)
Why: AI fabricates a founding myth from stock phrases when it has no facts.
Fix: real specifics — founders' names, the year, the triggering incident ("We built this after losing a $40k invoice in an email thread in 2023"), photos of actual humans.

**242. Phantom "team of experts"** (strong)
Tell: "Our team of experts is dedicated to your success", "world-class team of engineers" — yet no names, faces, or LinkedIn anywhere on the site.
Why: AI asserts institutional depth the site can't evidence.
Fix: name real people with real roles and links; a solo founder saying "It's just me — here's my GitHub" outperforms a fictional team.

## Blog & long-form

**243. AI blog scaffold** (strong)
Tell: posts opening "In this article, we'll explore...", an H2 every ~80 words, "Let's dive in", bold-header bullet stacks, closing "In conclusion" + "Remember, ..."
Why: the complete documented ChatGPT article skeleton; readers and Google both discount it.
Fix: one thesis per post, first-person experience, one real example with numbers; delete intro/outro paragraphs entirely — start at the point. (For deep prose de-sloping, the Anthropic `stop-slop` skill applies.)

**244. Fabricated blog citations** (strong)
Tell: "Studies show that 73% of marketers...", "According to industry reports..." with no link, or links to nonexistent studies; vague attribution ("Experts agree").
Why: hallucinated authority is a signature LLM failure; vague attribution is flagged weasel-wording.
Fix: link every stat to a named primary source and year, or cut it.

**245. Ghost bylines and same-day archives** (strong)
Tell: every post "By Admin" or authorless, 12 posts all dated the launch week, identical 5-min read times.
Why: a bulk-generated content calendar — the metadata exposes the batch job. (Site-wide missing history: #56.)
Fix: real author with bio and photo per post; publish on a human cadence; delete filler posts — 3 genuine articles beat 20 generated ones.

## Content hygiene

**246. Raw LLM artifacts in copy** (instant)
Tell: "Certainly! Here's a compelling headline for your landing page:", "As an AI language model...", "I hope this helps!" shipped verbatim.
Why: unedited chat transcript pasted into production — absolute proof of zero human review.
Fix: grep the built site for "Certainly", "Here's a", "As an AI", "I hope this helps", "Feel free to customize."

**247. Markdown bleed-through** (instant)
Tell: literal `**bold**` asterisks, `# headings`, or `[link text](url)` rendered as text on the live site.
Why: model output format leaking through the render pipeline.
Fix: grep rendered HTML text nodes for `**`, `](`, and backticks; fix the copy pipeline, not just the instance.

**248. Placeholder leftovers** (instant)
Tell: "Lorem ipsum...", "Your Company", john@example.com, (555) 123-4567, "123 Main Street", "[Insert testimonial here]", "Feature one description goes here."
Why: scaffold text the generator emitted and nobody replaced — the purest form of shipped slop.
Fix: pre-launch grep (and add it as a CI check): lorem, ipsum, example.com, "your company", "coming soon", TODO, [insert, {{, placeholder, "123 Main", "555".

**249. Product name drift** (strong)
Tell: hero calls it "TaskFlow", pricing calls it "Taskflow Pro", FAQ says "our platform", legal says another name entirely.
Why: sections generated in separate prompts against inconsistent context; no human read the page end-to-end.
Fix: single-source the product name; full-page read-aloud pass before launch; grep for old working titles.

**250. Stale or mismatched copyright line** (strong)
Tell: "© 2024 YourCompany. All rights reserved." in a later year, or a footer naming a different product than the header.
Why: fossilized template/generation date nobody proofread — instant "nobody lives here" signal.
Fix: dynamic year, correct legal entity name; read your own footer once.

**251. Template-tainted legal pages** (strong)
Tell: Privacy Policy/Terms containing "[Company Name]", the wrong company, boilerplate describing data the site never collects, no contact address.
Why: AI-generated legal text unread by its own publisher — a liability, not just a tell.
Fix: generate from a reputable policy service or counsel with the real entity, jurisdiction, and actual data practices; grep legal pages for brackets and other product names.
